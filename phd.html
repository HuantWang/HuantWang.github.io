Applying for a PhD with Me

A PhD takes three and a half to four years of your life on one or two highly specialised problems, so the first question to ask yourself is: why do you want one? There is no standard answer to this. What matters is that your answer is one that will keep supporting you through the whole of your PhD.

How I see my role

I am a fairly hands-on supervisor. I like to meet regularly, discuss your progress and future plans together, and give quick and honest feedback. I do not expect my students to be the smartest ones in the room. From my own experience, hard work, commitment, and having clear goals matter much more than raw intelligence. These are the qualities I value most in a PhD student. In return, I will do my best to help you achieve your goals. I deeply respect my students and their ideas. A PhD is your journey, and my job is to support you and help you become an independent researcher.

How it will go

  1. A short AI-assisted programming test. I will send you a simple "do at home" task, and you are encouraged to use AI assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot) — working effectively with them is part of what I want to see. It filters out mass-emailers and shows me how you approach a problem. No hard deadline, but how long you take counts.

  2. An online talk — not an interview. No grilling; we will chat about why you want a PhD, what ideas excite you, and about life: Leeds, funding, and what working with me day-to-day looks like. Use it to evaluate me too.

  3. The formal application and funding. If we are both happy, you apply through the University (transcripts, CV, references, and a short research proposal for self-proposed projects — I will help you polish these). Funding is available for competitive candidates — see the funding routes at the end of this page.

Entry requirements

Set by the School of Computer Science (I cannot waive them): a bachelor's degree with a 2:1 (Hons) or a good Master's in a relevant subject, and IELTS 6.5 overall with not less than 6.5 in writing and 6.0 in other components (or TOEFL iBT 88). International applicants may need an ATAS certificate.

Get in touch

If my research interests and publications grab you — or you have a great idea of your own — email me at scshwan@leeds.ac.uk with your CV and a short description of your background. I read every serious email.

Funding routes

Every scheme below is competitive, and overseas funding is more competitive still — but it is very often found for good students, and I will help you put the case together. Timing matters more than most people expect: most schemes close between December and March for an October start, so contact me the autumn before you want to begin.

Through Leeds

  • School of Computer Science Scholarship — the School's own competition, covering fees and a maintenance stipend. Open to international applicants.
  • Leeds Doctoral Scholarships — full fees plus a stipend at the standard UKRI rate (£20,780 in 2025/26) for 3.5 years. Only a handful each year, and UK fee-rated applicants only.
  • EPSRC Doctoral Landscape Awards and Centres for Doctoral Training — Research Council funded studentships, normally restricted to UK fee-rated applicants.
  • Funded project studentships — posts advertised against a specific project or grant. These come and go through the year; a few are open to international applicants.

For up-to-date details, see the School's funding and fees page.

External and national sponsors

  • China Scholarship Council (CSC) — the main route for applicants from China. Apply to CSC in March–April, so get in touch well before then.
  • Commonwealth PhD Scholarships — for eligible low- and middle-income Commonwealth countries.
  • National government schemes — worth checking whether your own government, or a company in your country, runs one.
  • Self- or employer-funded, with a 10% alumni fee discount for Leeds graduates, and dedicated funding for under-represented groups.
